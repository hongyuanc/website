# Product

## Register

Personal portfolio / quiet digital home.

## Users

The primary audience is anyone trying to get a clearer sense of Hong Yuan Cao as a person who builds software: peers, friends, collaborators, engineers, professors, recruiters, and hiring managers. Recruiters should still be able to understand the work quickly, but the site should not feel built around being evaluated by them.

Visitors arrive with different levels of context. Some need the resume and project links fast. Others may be browsing out of curiosity. Both groups should leave with a grounded impression of Hong's current chapter, interests, technical taste, and standards without feeling like they have been pushed through a recruiting funnel.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio should feel like a considered personal index: a simple place to understand what Hong is doing, what he has built, what he is learning, and how his interests connect across software engineering, machine learning, systems, computer vision, and economics.

The site is not primarily a conversion page. Success means a visitor can understand Hong's trajectory, inspect representative work, access the resume, and leave with a credible impression of craft, restraint, and personality. It should be useful to a recruiter without sounding like it was written only for recruiting.

## Positioning

Lead with the person, not the pitch. The homepage should feel like a calm introduction rather than a hero section trying to prove value in ten seconds. Project details should act as evidence of judgment and curiosity, not trophies arranged for maximum hiring appeal. The resume should remain easy to reach, but it should not dominate the experience.

Personal texture should come from small, specific choices: a plainspoken introduction, a careful project selection, cross-disciplinary interests, and a few human details that explain what Hong pays attention to. Avoid diary-like oversharing, but allow the site to feel lived-in.

## Brand Personality

Personal, elegant, minimal, subtle, and self-aware. The voice should be plainspoken and precise, with enough warmth to feel human. It should feel confident because it is calm, not because it is loud.

The site should avoid cheap polish, generic portfolio theatrics, and copy that sounds like begging for employment. It should also avoid the opposite problem: becoming so minimal that it feels blank, anonymous, or unfinished.

## Anti-references

This should not look like a cheap template portfolio, a generic SaaS landing page, a loud recruiting funnel, or a page overloaded with badges, metrics, social proof, and calls to hire. Avoid stock job-seeker language, exaggerated personal-brand copy, corny taglines, and anything that reads as trying too hard.

Avoid novelty 3D as the main organizing idea. The default redesign direction should remove the cube as the primary interface, since it can block natural reading, make navigation feel awkward, hide content, create browser-specific issues, and become the thing people remember more than the work. A subtle interaction is acceptable only if it supports the content and disappears when it is not useful.

## Design Principles

Make the site feel like an edited personal index, not a landing page.

Signal earned capability through specific projects, concise technical detail, and visible care in the interface.

Use minimalism as a mark of discipline: enough hierarchy, contrast, and spacing to feel intentional, never thin or unfinished.

Keep the site personal without turning it into a diary, a self-promotional campaign, or a resume wrapper.

Let motion and interaction orient the visitor, not compete with the content.

Prefer simple, direct navigation over an imposed visual metaphor. The experience should work as a normal page before it works as an animation.

Make the resume and project paths easy to reach while keeping the overall tone calm and unneedy.

## Content Principles

Use first person sparingly when it makes the writing more natural. Do not over-script the voice into a brand persona.

Keep the homepage concise: name, current chapter, a short human introduction, and clear paths into work, resume, and contact.

Describe projects as records of what was made, why it mattered, and what technical choices were involved. Avoid inflated impact claims unless they are concrete and verifiable.

Let personal blend show through the selection and ordering of content: systems work next to ML tooling, coursework next to projects, economics and writing as part of the background rather than unrelated extras.

Keep contact and recruiting affordances available, but visually quiet. No "hire me" posture, no over-eager CTA stack, and no repeated reminders that the visitor should evaluate Hong.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus states, and readable structure. Preserve reduced-motion alternatives for 3D transitions, page changes, and hover effects. Keep text legible across mobile and desktop, avoid relying on color alone, and ensure resume access has both preview and downloadable paths.
